# Content type: how-to

This page walks the reader through doing something, in order, and it is judged on
whether they can follow it.

**Sections are stages of the task, in the order the reader performs them.** Not
themes, not benefits. "Disassembling the pump" comes before "Cleaning the
impeller" because that is the order of the work. One short opening section may
say why the task matters; everything after it is the task.

**A step that needs a quantity names it as something the author must supply.**
Soak times, dilution ratios, intervals and torque settings are real and specific,
and you do not know them. Write the `purpose` as an instruction to the author:
"Record how long parts soak and in what dilution" is useful. "Soak for fifteen
minutes" is a fabrication unless the facts say it. The draft is checked against
the facts and a number that is not in them fails the page, so inventing one costs
the author a rerun.

**Name the failure the reader will hit.** A how-to that only describes the happy
path is the one competitors already wrote. If the research carries a problem
("not working", "leaking", "green residue"), give it a section or an H3, because
that is what the searcher who is mid-task actually needs.

**Do not sell.** A how-to earns trust by being usable; a product recommendation
inside it reads as the reason the page exists. Keep any product mention to the
supplies the task needs, and only where the research or the facts name them.

Everything else is unchanged: `source` still traces to the research, and a stage
of the task the research gives no basis for is left out rather than invented.

**The call to action is the next step of the task**, or what to do when it is
done: run the fountain for an hour and check the flow, note the date for the next
clean. Not a purchase, unless the task genuinely needs a part the reader lacks.

**The facts of a how-to are the quantities the steps need**: soak times, dilution
ratios, intervals, the size of the brush. Those are what the author knows and the
model does not, and a step that needs one and lacks it is the step that gets
invented.
