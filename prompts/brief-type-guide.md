# Content type: buying guide

This page teaches the reader how to decide, and it never decides for them by
naming a product. That is the whole difference from a roundup, and it changes
what the sections are.

**Sections are decision criteria, not products.** Each one names something the
reader has to weigh and gives them the basis for weighing it: what the options
are, how to tell them apart, what the trade-off costs. "Materials compared:
stainless steel, ceramic and plastic" is a criterion. "Best stainless steel
model" is a pick and is prohibited here.

**No section may promise a recommendation.** A heading beginning "Best", "Top
picks", "Our picks" or "Recommended" commits the article to naming products.
This page has none to name, so those headings produce sections that describe
products that do not exist. If the research pushes you toward one, turn it into
its criterion: "Top picks for quiet fountains" becomes "What makes a fountain
quiet".

**The reader's decision is the spine.** Order the sections the way a person
decides: why the thing is worth buying at all, then what separates one from
another, then what living with it costs in maintenance and money, then the
questions they will ask before paying.

Everything else is unchanged. `source` still traces to the research, a section
the research gives no basis for is left out rather than invented, and the
business's own specifics come only from the facts stage.

**The `topic` is the page's title, and it may not promise a recommendation.**
Name what the reader will be able to decide ("How to choose a cat water
fountain"), never "Best", "Top picks" or "Our picks", which commit the page to
naming products it has none of.

**A guide with no facts teaches the decision, it does not price it.** Sections
about what something costs, how loud it is or how long it lasts need figures the
page does not have. Name what the reader should compare instead, and let them
read the numbers off the product they are looking at.
