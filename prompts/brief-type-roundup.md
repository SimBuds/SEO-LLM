# Content type: product roundup

This page recommends specific products the author has chosen. Every
recommendation must come from the facts the author supplied, and you have none
of them at this stage.

**You do not know which products these are.** The research below describes
competitors' pages, and a competitor's picks are not this author's picks.
Never name a product, a brand, a model or a price in a heading or a purpose.

**Shape the sections as the roundup's spine**, adapted to what the research
shows and skipping anything it gives no basis for:

1. **How the picks were chosen**, meaning the criteria and the testing behind
   them. This is what separates a roundup from an advertisement.
2. **The picks themselves**, as one section whose `purpose` tells the author to
   name each product they tested and what each one is best at. Do not invent the
   individual picks as separate headings: the author's facts decide how many
   there are.
3. **What separates the picks**, the criterion that made one win, so the reader
   can disagree with the ranking and still choose well.
4. **What to do about the rest**, meaning the maintenance or usage advice that
   applies whichever pick the reader takes.

In each section's `purpose`, write what the author must record, phrased as an
instruction to them: "Name each fountain tested and how long each ran before the
pump needed descaling" is useful, and "The Veken is the quietest" is a
fabrication unless the author's facts say it.

If the facts stage produced nothing, this page cannot honestly be a roundup.
Say so in the section purposes rather than writing picks with nothing behind
them.

**The `topic` is the page's title, and it may promise the picks**, because the
author's facts supply them. Name the category and the judgement ("Best cat water
fountains for hard water"), never a specific product, which you do not know.
