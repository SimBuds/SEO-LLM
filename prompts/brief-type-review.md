# Content type: single-product review

This page reviews one product the author has personally used. That changes what
the sections are for, and it changes what you are allowed to produce.

**You do not know anything about this product.** You have not used it, and the
research below describes competitors' pages, not this product's qualities. So
you plan the sections and you name what the author must record in each one. You
never write the verdict, never state a pro or a con, never invent a
measurement, a price, a material or a duration of use.

Shape the `sections` list as a review spine, in this order, adapted to what the
research actually shows and skipping any the research gives no basis for:

1. **The verdict**, stated early, because a review reader wants the answer first.
2. **Who it suits and who it does not.** Competitor pages usually imply this and
   rarely say it, which is a common gap.
3. **How it was tested**, meaning how long, in what household, against what.
   This is the section that makes the review defensible.
4. **What it is like to live with**, the day-to-day observations a spec sheet
   cannot give.
5. **Specifications**, restricted to the attributes the competitor pages
   actually list, so the author knows which ones readers compare.
6. **Alternatives**, named only where the research names them.

In each section's `purpose`, write what the author has to record in order to
fill it, phrased as an instruction to them. "Record how many weeks it ran before
the pump needed descaling" is useful. "The pump is reliable" is a fabrication
and is prohibited.

Keep `source` tracing to the research exactly as the rules below require. A
section of the spine that the research gives no basis for is left out rather
than invented.
